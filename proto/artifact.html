Three lenses on a rim

A planet drawn as a cross-section: one circle, and everything that matters happens on its edge. Terrain, ice, forest, coastline and cities all live in the few pixels either side of the ground line, and the interior is deliberately shadow.

“2D circles, things happen in the edge. Scale should be skewed so that I can see the whole planet and also see surface details.”

Same world, same paint, same drawing pass in all three plates. The only thing that changes is the lens — how a point's longitude and altitude become a place on the screen. Drag any plate to travel round the rim; all three stay locked to the same spot.

Exaggerate the altitude

Rim

One true circle. Longitude is honest all the way round; altitude is not. The forty metres above sea level get as much screen as the five hundred kilometres below it, so mountains, ice, forest and coastline all read at a glance with the whole planet in frame.

A town narrower than three pixels is drawn as a mark rather than as geometry. A world has to look inhabited from orbit.

Take relief to zero to see what the skew buys: at true scale the planet is a plain circle with a hairline of sky. Take zoom up to see the cost of having no lens at all — detail only arrives once the planet has left the frame.

Redistribute the longitude

Fisheye

The disc never changes size and never leaves the screen. A window of ground around the focus is spread across a fixed arc, and the entire rest of the planet is compressed logarithmically into what is left — so every decade of distance from where you are looking costs the same slice of screen.

The falloff is solved, not tuned: it is the one value that makes the angular scale continuous across the edge of the window. Guess, and every coastline crossing that edge has a visible kink in it.

This is the plate that answers the brief literally — a street and the far side of the world in one unbroken circle. Its limit is curvature: the focus window may only hold a shallow arc, because a street bent through 110° splays its buildings out like the teeth of a crown.

Repeat the circle

Ladder

The planet stays a small undistorted disc. Each ring outside it is one slice of the ring within, unrolled and magnified by the same factor — so the marked wedge is always one part in step of whatever it sits on, and the plumb line says all four are looking at the same place.

Ground sits on each ring's inner edge with the sky filling outward, so up is outward everywhere, the planet included. Each rung carries its own magnification and span.

Every scale on screen at once, and nothing distorted anywhere — but each rung is a thin band, and you read it rather than fly through it. This is the instrument, not the window.

What the three of them settle

Three things came out of building them that were not obvious from the sketch, and all three are about where the skew is allowed to act.

Heights measure from their own ground
Feeding absolute altitude through a locally true scale put a town sitting at 400 m elevation 180 px outside the disc, its buildings floating in a sky above the coastline they belonged to. Terrain keeps the art-directed ramp — that is the skew, and it is what makes a mountain legible from orbit — while an object's own height takes whichever is larger, the ramp or a locally true scale.
Magnification is a ground-level effect
Applied at every altitude, it ran away with the sky: the 130 km shell got pushed out past 1.5× the disc wherever the lens magnified, so the atmosphere came out as a lobe instead of a circle. Full strength under 600 m, gone by 6 km.
The interior is shadow
Concentric bands of rock were the whole problem with the old planet: the eye went to the middle, where nothing happens, and the rim — where everything happens — was a hairline. The body is now one value barely above the void, the caption in the middle says what the world is, and the rim is the only lit thing in the picture.

Where I would take it: Rim is the planet rung, and it is finished enough to build against. Fisheye is the answer for the descent — it is the only one of the three with no cut in it — provided the focus window stays a shallow arc. Ladder is a fine instrument and a poor vehicle; its idea worth keeping is the labelled rung, not the rings.
